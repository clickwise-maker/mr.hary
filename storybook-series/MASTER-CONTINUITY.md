# Children's Fantasy Storybook Series — Master Continuity
**Status:** Concept baseline / continuity source of truth  
**Repository:** `clickwise-maker/mr.hary`  
**Audience:** Children ages 4–8  
**Last updated:** 2026-10-17

> This file is the memory checkpoint for the story series. Before writing or generating a new page, read this file. After a story is completed or a major decision is approved, update the relevant book state and series roadmap in the same repository.

## 1. Core product concept

Create an original, child-friendly fantasy-adventure storybook series combining:
- Color comic-style story scenes that move the story forward.
- Black-and-white sketch/coloring pages.
- Simple age-appropriate puzzles and observation activities woven into the narrative.
- Visual “find the differences/mistakes” activities introduced gently and made more challenging only later in the series.
- A suspenseful ending/cliffhanger that makes children want the next book.

This is **not** just a coloring book or a standalone maths workbook. Story, illustrations and activities must feel like one connected adventure.

## 2. Book 1 — current story canon

**Working title:** *The Mystery of the Magic Egg* (title can be finalized later).  
**Main plot:** A magical waterfall has mysteriously dried up. Mia and Leo ask their Grandpa for help. Grandpa has an old book/map that points them toward the Whispering Forest. Mia and Leo explore, follow clues and cross stepping stones. Beneath a giant tree, they discover a glowing magical egg. The egg begins to tap and crack, but the reveal is held back as a cliffhanger for the next installment.

### Required story beats
1. Introduce Mia, Leo and Grandpa in a warm, loving family setting.
2. Show the once-magical waterfall now dry; make the mystery visually clear to young readers.
3. Grandpa reveals an old book/map and points the children toward the Whispering Forest.
4. The children enter the forest and encounter simple clues.
5. Include a stepping-stone/sequence challenge as part of the journey.
6. They discover the glowing egg beneath a giant tree.
7. End with the egg tapping/cracking and a short mysterious clue or sound. Do not fully reveal the baby dragon too early; the cliffhanger should excite children about Book 2.

**Important continuity:** Do not replace this current plot with the older “missing rainbow / seven rainbow kingdoms” draft. An earlier file at `storybook-test-001/` is only a 5-page interaction prototype and currently contains a different placeholder story. Keep it as a prototype/reference for interaction mechanics, not as the canonical Book 1 plot. When developing the actual book, align it with the waterfall-and-magic-egg story above.

## 3. Character continuity

- **Mia:** Curious, observant and brave; asks questions and notices small details.
- **Leo:** Mia’s adventure partner; enjoys working through clues and puzzles with her.
- **Grandpa:** Warm, supportive and wise; owns the old book/map that begins the quest.
- **Baby dragon (future reveal):** Cute, friendly and non-threatening. Current visual direction: chubby body, turquoise/sky-blue scales, sunny yellow belly, rounded coral-orange horns, small wings with warm orange-pink membranes, large sparkling teal eyes, rosy cheeks, small feet and arms, short curled tail with a rainbow-colored tuft, tiny harmless fang and happy grin. Keep its silhouette, colors and facial features consistent across future illustrations.

Do not introduce extra major characters or change established character designs without recording the decision here first.

## 4. Page and print-design rules

- Target reader: ages 4–8. Use short sentences, clear actions, warm humor and easy-to-follow suspense.
- Use **10 color pages** and make the remaining interior pages black-and-white to help control print cost. Treat the cover as a separate production item unless the user explicitly decides that it counts toward the ten pages.
- Place color pages at key emotional/story highlights, not randomly.
- Between story scenes, alternate suitable black-and-white activity pages: sketch/color prompts, simple puzzles, sequencing, counting, and observation. Activities must relate to what just happened in the story.
- Introduce “find the mistakes/differences” at an easy level first; increase difficulty gradually across later books.
- Keep enough white space and line clarity for home printing and children to write or color.
- Final page count, trim size, bleed, and exact page-by-page color allocation are **not locked yet**. Do not claim a final print-ready PDF exists until it has actually been created and checked.

## 5. Book production and sales plan

Create one master story/design, then prepare format-specific outputs:
1. **Amazon KDP paperback:** print-ready interior PDF plus cover file, checked against KDP's current requirements.
2. **Amazon Kindle eBook:** a Kindle-suitable digital edition, with layout tested for the comic/activity format.
3. **Payhip digital product:** downloadable PDF suitable for viewing and home printing.

The story and core artwork should remain consistent across editions, but file layouts may differ by platform. If selling the same digital book on Payhip as well as Kindle, do **not** enroll that eBook in KDP Select/Kindle Unlimited exclusivity. Verify current platform rules before publishing.

## 6. Series roadmap (tentative; do not treat future titles as locked)

- **Book 1:** The dried magical waterfall; Grandpa's old map; Whispering Forest; glowing egg cliffhanger.
- **Book 2:** Continue directly from the egg cracking; reveal the baby dragon and establish why the waterfall stopped flowing.
- **Books 3–10:** Continue the larger fantasy mystery with new missions, locations and progressively richer observation/logic activities. Brainstorm future titles only after the Book 1–2 story continuity is stable; record approved titles and plot turns here before drafting them.

Keep all characters, names, plots and artwork original. Use broad genre inspiration only; do not copy existing franchise characters, signature settings or plots.

## 7. Required workflow after every story

1. Read this master continuity file and the previous book's completed state before starting.
2. Plan the next story beats and activity sequence; preserve the approved story canon.
3. Draft the story and page-by-page plan.
4. Review age suitability, narrative flow, puzzle answers, originality, and character consistency.
5. Complete and verify the story before marking it complete.
6. Commit the finished story files to this repository.
7. Update this file with: what happened, what was revealed, unresolved mysteries, new characters/objects, puzzle skills used, and the exact hook for the next book.
8. Start the next story only from the saved continuity checkpoint—not from memory or a fresh guess.
