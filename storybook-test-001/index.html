The morning the rainbow vanished

Mia loved the rainbow that appeared above Willowbell every morning. Her best friend Leo liked solving mysteries, and Pip the fox liked snacks almost as much as secrets.

But today, the sky was grey. The rainbow had disappeared—and every crystal in the old tower had gone dull.

Mia: “Look! A tiny light is blinking inside the old map!”
Leo: “Then we follow the clues. Together.”

Read aloud together. What do you think made the rainbow disappear?

A map with a missing piece

The map showed seven kingdoms, but the first path was hidden. Three symbols glowed around its edge: a moon, a star and a leaf.

“The map wants us to notice something,” Leo said. Pip sniffed the paper. “I notice it smells like biscuits.”

Story clue: The right path is marked by the symbol that shines at night.

Which symbol should Mia choose?

The gate needs a number code. Mia finds 7 blue crystals and Leo finds 5 golden crystals. How many crystals do they have altogether?

Adult note: Encourage the child to count, draw dots or use fingers. The answer is part of the story—not a speed test.

04
Look closely · Find the clue

Which picture has the hidden clue?

The map room has three tiny pictures. Only one shows the same star pattern as the old map. Look carefully before you choose.

Observation skill: Compare the order of the symbols from left to right.

A voice in the Whispering Forest

The gate opened with a soft chime. Beyond it, a forest shimmered in silver and green. From somewhere among the trees, a small voice whispered:

Unknown voice: “If you want the rainbow back, find the friend who has forgotten their colour…”

Mia, Leo and Pip stepped beneath the first tree. The leaves rustled—even though there was no wind.

Mission complete!

You found clues, solved a number puzzle and studied a pattern. In the full book, each mission will reveal a piece of the larger mystery.

Prototype note: This is a 5-page interaction test, not the complete 70–75-page book. Names, art and story are original draft material and can be revised.

Prototype for testing story flow, child-friendly puzzles and low-cost print layout.
